# History and Debates

> **Where the evidence is contested, we say so. Where it is absent, we say "unknown".**

---

## 1. The short version

What can actually be established about Tieban Shenshu is **very little**:

| Question | Status |
|---|---|
| Composed by Shao Yong? | **No** — challenged since the Ming; no Song-dynasty evidence |
| Earliest appearance | Traceable sources point to the **mid-Qing** |
| First compiler | **Unknown** |
| Did "Tie Buzi" exist? | **Unknown** — no reliable source |
| Origin of the "12,000 verses" figure | **Unknown** |
| Manuscript stemma | **Unknown** |

## 3. The key evidence: Yang Shen's criticism

Yang Shen (楊慎, 1488–1559), in *Sheng'an ji* (《升庵集》):

> "Nowadays wandering diviners recklessly fabricate the *Dading shu* and *Chunzi shu*,
> falsely attributing them to Kangjie — is this not a grave slander of a former worthy?"

### What this proves

- By the **mid-16th century**, systems attributed to Shao Yong were circulating
- A prominent scholar had **publicly called this fabrication**
- The tradition is therefore older than the Qing, and had already been challenged

### What this does not prove

- That *Dading shu*/*Chunzi shu* **are** Tieban Shenshu (only that they are cognate)
- That Shao Yong had no connection at all ("fabrication" is Yang Shen's judgement)
- A precise date

> Evidence level: **classical text** (the quotation) + **scholarly discussion** (the inference)

## 4. Shao Yong and the *Huangji Jingshi*

> Want the full treatment? See [What is Huangji Jingshi](huangji-jingshi.md).

**Shao Yong** (邵雍, 1011–1077), styled Yaofu, posthumous name Kangjie. Northern Song
cosmologist and Yijing scholar; one of the "Five Masters of Northern Song".

**The *Huangji Jingshi* (皇極經世)** computes historical and cosmic cycles:

```
1 yuan (元) = 12 hui (會)
1 hui       = 30 yun (運)
1 yun       = 12 shi (世)
1 shi       = 30 years
→ 1 yuan    = 129,600 years
```

This is a **historical-philosophical** framework, not a method for individual fortune-telling.

### Relationship to Tieban Shenshu

| Claim | Assessment |
|---|---|
| "Tieban Shenshu derives from the *Huangji Jingshi*" | **Traditional account**, unproven |
| "It borrows some numerological concepts" | **Plausible**, extent unclear |
| "They are the same system" | **Wrong** — different objects, units, and purposes |

## 5. The "Tie Buzi" problem

Tradition says a figure styled "Tie Buzi" (鐵卜子) revised the corpus in the Qianlong–Jiaqing era.

| Question | Status |
|---|---|
| Real name | Unknown ("Tie Buzi" is a sobriquet) |
| Dates | Unknown |
| Native place | Unknown |
| Other works | Unknown |
| Mentioned by contemporaries | **No** |
| Epitaph, genealogy, local gazetteer | **None found** |

**A person said to have revised twelve thousand verses, yet invisible in contemporaneous records,
is inherently suspicious.**

> Evidence level: **traditional account**. We neither affirm nor disprove, but do not present it as fact.

## 6. The "12,000 verses" figure

| Question | Status |
|---|---|
| Why exactly 12,000? | Unknown — possibly a neat 12 volumes × 1000 design |
| Do all versions agree? | **No** — counts differ between manuscripts and transcription projects |
| Duplicates or omissions? | Likely, but no systematic study exists |
| Who fixed the number? | Unknown |

Our tool's `check` command uses 1001–13000 purely as a **demonstration range**.
It is not a claim about any particular edition.

## 7. Regional transmission

**Hong Kong** became a significant hub for publishing and teaching divination in the late
20th century; some Tieban Shenshu material reached overseas Chinese communities through it.

**We do not claim** that Hong Kong is the *origin*. It is a **transmission hub**, not a birthplace.

**Taiwan** likewise has active publishing and teaching under the name 鐵板神數.

**Macau, Southeast Asia, and Western Chinese communities** also have traditions,
but systematically documented public records are scarce.

> Evidence level: **scholarly discussion**. Regional transmission history lacks systematic study.

---

## 8. The digitisation problem

Multiple public projects have digitised verse texts. This creates new problems:

| Problem | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Different base editions | Content diverges |
| Uneven transcription quality | Typos, missing lines, misordering |
| Some projects have no licence | Unclear legal status |
| Some forbid commercial use | Unusable for commercial products |
| No collation standard | No way to judge which is "more correct" |

**Therefore**: the reliability of "the complete Tieban Shenshu verses" found online
depends on transcription quality **you cannot verify**.

We consequently adopt a conservative policy: **we copy no unattributed transcription.**

---

## 9. Our position

As a **historical text**: worth studying — it reflects Qing-era popular ideas about fate, family,
and ethics.

As a **predictive method**: unverifiable. No reproducible, peer-reviewed study supports its efficacy.

As a **commercial product**: has a market, but should describe its limits honestly.

As a **cultural phenomenon**: it links Yijing tradition, folk belief, publishing, and modern
social psychology — a legitimate object of study in itself.
