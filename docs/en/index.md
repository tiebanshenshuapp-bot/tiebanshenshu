# Tieban Shenshu — Overview

> This is introductory material from our public research community.
> Every claim is labelled with an **evidence level**. See [section 5](#5-evidence-levels).

---

## 1. What it is

**Tieban Shenshu** (鐵板神數 / 铁板神数, also written 鐵版神數; literally "Iron Plate Divine Numbers")
is a Chinese divination system.

Three defining features:

1. **Number-to-verse lookup** — a birth time is converted into a number, and that number indexes a
   fixed verse (條文 / *tiaowen*).
2. **Kaoke / dingfen** (考刻定分) — narrowing the birth hour down to a *ke* (about 15 minutes) and
   further to a *fen*, using family information as cross-checks.
3. **Verse corpus format** — the output is a numbered set of verses, traditionally said to number
   around twelve thousand.

---

## 2. How it differs from BaZi

> Full comparison (including Zi Wei Dou Shu): [Three systems compared](comparison.md).

People often conflate Tieban Shenshu with BaZi (八字, the Four Pillars system). They are not the same:

| Dimension | BaZi (子平術) | Tieban Shenshu |
|---|---|---|
| Core method | Four pillars → ten gods → assess strength and favourability | Four pillars → derive number → look up verse ID |
| Output form | Analytical prose | Fixed, numbered verses |
| Key step | Choosing the useful god; luck cycles | Kaoke / dingfen |
| Basis of judgement | Structure of the chart | Content of the verse |

**Key point**: the primary output of Tieban Shenshu is a **verse**, not a structural analysis.
Explaining Tieban Shenshu through BaZi theory is a category error.

> Evidence level: **scholarly discussion.** Researchers disagree on the precise relationship,
> but treating the two methods as equivalent is a clear misunderstanding.

## 3. Origins: what is clear and what is not

### What is clear

**The attribution to Shao Yong is a traditional claim with no Song-dynasty textual support.**

As early as the Ming dynasty, Yang Shen (楊慎, 1488–1559) wrote in *Sheng'an ji* (《升庵集》):

> "Nowadays wandering diviners recklessly fabricate the *Dading shu* and *Chunzi shu*,
> falsely attributing them to Kangjie [Shao Yong] — is this not a grave slander of a former worthy?"

So by the **mid-Ming at the latest**, the attribution itself was already being challenged by a
respected scholar.

**Emergence in the mid-Qing, associated with "Tie Buzi".**

Tradition holds that during the Qianlong–Jiaqing period (1736–1820) a figure styled
"Tie Buzi" (鐵卜子, "Iron Diviner") or "Tie Daoren" revised the verses, while still honouring
Shao Yong as the original author and describing himself only as an editor.

> Evidence level: **traditional account.** There is no reliable primary source for Tie Buzi's
> identity, dates, or even existence.

### What is not clear

- Did the method exist in the Song or Yuan? No definite description survives.
- Who first compiled the verses? Unknown.
- When did "twelve thousand verses" become the standard figure? Unknown.
- What is the manuscript stemma? Unknown.

## 4. What we do **not** claim

| ❌ We do not claim | Why |
|---|---|
| "Personally transmitted by Shao Yong" | No Song-dynasty evidence; challenged since the Ming |
| "An imperially guarded secret" | No historical basis |
| "We are the only authentic lineage" | The transmission question is unresolved; nobody can prove it |
| "Accuracy of XX%" | Not verifiable by any reproducible method |
| "Learning this lets you change your fate" | Outside the scope of textual research; misleading |
| "Hong Kong is the true birthplace" | No evidence supports such a definite claim |

## 7. A word of caution

Tieban Shenshu is a **historical text** worth studying as a cultural phenomenon.

It is not an instruction manual for your life. Please do not base medical, financial, legal, or
major life decisions on it.

## 8. For international readers

A few notes that often cause confusion:

**On the name.** "Tieban Shenshu" is a romanisation of 鐵板神數. You will also see
"Tie Ban Shen Shu", "Tieban Shenshu", "Tiepan Shenshu", and "Iron Plate Divine Numbers".
The last is a literal translation, not an established academic term.

**On "authenticity".** There is no critical edition, no authoritative lineage register, and no
scholarly consensus on origins. Treat any claim of a single authentic transmission with caution.

**On terminology.** Chinese terms are given in the original with an explanation, rather than
translated by invented English equivalents. *Kaoke* (考刻) and *dingfen* (定分) have no standard
English renderings; inventing one would obscure more than it clarifies.

**On scope.** This is not a divination service. It is a documentation and education project.
